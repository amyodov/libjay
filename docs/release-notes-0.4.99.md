This release carries the J push most of the way to its milestone without claiming it; the number says how close. The 0.5.0 gate is zero unexplained disagreements with the reference interpreter; two sweeps of fifty thousand generated expressions against jconsole now read 99.987% accepted-adjusted agreement with eight unexplained rows apiece, down from twenty-five at 0.4.9, and that mark has held for twelve consecutive pairs. Beside the coverage, three performance rounds took the interpreter's cost out of every explicit definition, and six number-crunching algorithms arrived written in J with numpy and numba references beside them.

The agreement loop changed method. Sampling a finite table of primitive × valence × value class a few cells at a time had plateaued at twenty-odd unexplained rows per sweep, so the table was read whole instead: a `jay-corpus grid j` subcommand generates every sentence of every verb and modifier over twenty-eight special-value classes and three shapes, runs both engines, and records the agreeing cells as corpus. Four such grids, about a quarter of a million measured cells, cover the primitives, the modifiers, the fold and dyadic forms and the compositions, the obverses, the explicit bodies and the representations. What the grids left was then taken by mechanism, never by row: each residue round clustered the remaining disagreements by the rule beneath them, measured that rule's whole family against the reference, and fixed the generalization. Forty such rules landed, among them the type an empty frame's fill run carries, an obverse the reference does not define being a domain error rather than a gap, a negative cycle index counting from the end, the binomial of a close pair as one gamma ratio, the `s:` symbol forms in full, and the parts of a sentence a scalar primitive settles once per pass. Ten earlier pins were reversed on proof and became agreeing corpus rows.

Every pinned divergence now carries its reason. The list holds 244 rows under five tags: the reference contradicts itself between neighbouring spellings, the reference is proven wrong against the mathematics with the proof beside it, the Dictionary leaves the case undefined, the reference aborts or prints nothing, or the difference is a documented model choice, which is the thirteen rows where J's three character widths meet libjay's one. A family rule matches the same divergence by cause across the spellings that reach it, and a rule may widen only on a measured cell. The sweep tool gained a per-sentence wall clock that kills a busy worker on the oldest unanswered sentence, and two excuse classes for answers the reference abbreviates or reads below the printed tolerance.

Performance took three rounds against the profile rather than the benchmark. The first fused the non-associative infix reductions, the windowed correlation and the typed fold with a primitive step into single passes, so `2 -/\ y` over a million doubles went from 481 milliseconds to the reference's one, and the correlation from 1686 to 18 against the reference's 465. The second put an atom's buffer inline, made a call's locals a kept frame of slots, and let an atom against an atom skip the rank machinery. The third gave an array of rank two or less its shape in the header and cut that header from 104 bytes to 80, since every remaining allocation in a fold's profile was a shape vector. A fold with a defined dyad step over a million items went from 2884 to 1432 milliseconds against the reference's 442; KAMA from 2800 to 1529, Hurst from 404 to 296. The Savitzky-Golay filter runs in 18 milliseconds against the reference's 486 and Hopfield recall in 174 against 681. None of it is hand-written SIMD; every kernel is a plain typed loop the backend vectorises, and a one-codegen-unit build was tried and reverted because it slowed those loops by a third.

Six algorithms live in `examples/algos`, each written in J from its own mathematics and checked three ways: KAMA, a variance ratio, Hurst rescaled range, a Savitzky-Golay filter, an extreme learning machine, and Hopfield recall. Each agrees with its numpy reference and with jconsole within a stated tolerance, in the Python tests and in the recorded corpus, and `docs/algorithms.md` carries the kernel and algorithm timings against jconsole, numpy and numba with the findings each round measured.

What stays open is named, and it is what stands between this release and 0.5.0. The fold conjunctions cannot be checked against the reference because they hang it, so their rows rest on the non-fold spelling of the same step. The obverse of `x ^ x` is a Lambert W the Dictionary's table does not list. The binary representation of a symbol is the interpreter's own table slot. The evaluator still walks its expression tree, which leaves a defined-step fold about three times the reference; closing that needs a body compiled once, a round of its own.

Nothing in the public API is displaced. The Python, Rust and C surfaces are unchanged; the array header's narrowing is internal, and every addition is language coverage behind the same string.
