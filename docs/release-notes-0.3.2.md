This release is the harvest of a week of systematic hunting: three documentation crawls (the complete J Dictionary, the GNU APL manual, the Dyalog language reference), two wild-hunt passes over real-world code, and a rebuilt perpetual fuzzer. Around fifty-five register tickets were closed, and the recorded differential corpus grew by roughly 3,200 expressions to about 13,000.

Three panics are gone: `+//.` over an empty matrix, a sparse array under `u^:(n0 n1)` (which built a malformed array in release builds and crashed later), and their relatives, all fixed at the shared root rather than patched per spelling.

A batch of silent wrong answers is fixed. In J: `u~` reported no ranks so outer products over commuted verbs collapsed to an elementwise pass; dyadic `u^:_n` applied the verb forward instead of through its obverse (this one also affected APL's `⍣¯n`); sorting boxed values compared shape before contents, so grading a list of words was wrong; monadic `u . v` handed the operand ones instead of values; NaN comparison, matching and grading now follow what jconsole actually does, with the J Dictionary's own "results may not be correct or consistent" list cited where jconsole contradicts itself. In APL: `12 ⊤≠ 10` answered 1 instead of 6 — the whole bitwise `⊤`-family is now implemented.

J string literals are now byte vectors, as the Dictionary specifies: `# 'héllo'` is 6, `a. i. 'é'` is the two UTF-8 bytes, and the mojibake a terminal shows for a widened byte agrees with jconsole's byte for byte. The old Unicode reading survives behind the first entry of a new opt-in extension mechanism: `LIBJAY_J_UNICODE_STRINGS=1` in the environment sets a process default, and every surface — Rust, Python, CLI, C ABI — can override it per compiler. Extensions are documented apart from dialects in docs/extensions.md, and none of them is ever recorded against the oracle corpus.

The rest of the harvest, briefly: labels and branching in APL `∇` definitions (the classical loop idiom), `→→ ←→ ←←` conditionals, distributed assignment `(a b)←values`, double-quoted strings with C escapes, hexadecimal `$ff` literals, the matrix product `A∘B`, character ordering under `< ≤ ≥ >`, Dyalog's `@` operator, `[1]`-numbered `∇` bodies, J's constant verb `m"n`, the noun definition `0 : 0`, the monad-dyad conjunction `u : v`, complex copies-and-fills counts for `#`, lists of index specifications and gerunds in `}`, gerunds under `/ \ \. /.`, exact `q:` over extendeds with Miller–Rabin and Pollard's rho, rational bases in `b`-literals, and Dyalog's short left arguments to `↑`/`↓`, major-cell `≠`, general integer expand, and dyadic `⍕` forms — with six new dialect settings capturing where GNU APL and Dyalog genuinely part ways.

The Dyalog preset now matches 2,544 of 2,585 recorded Dyalog answers; the exemption ledger is unchanged at 41 rows. The J Dictionary crawl is complete at 114 of 114 pages, and it caught the Dictionary being stale on five pages — J 9.6.3 dropped the derivative conjunctions and repurposed `t.`/`T.`, and libjay follows the living interpreter, not the stale page.

Nothing in the public API is displaced; the `extensions` parameter and the C ABI's `jay_compile_ext` are additions beside the existing surface.
